Watch out!
  • Nationalities always start with a capital letter: Japanese food, not japanese food.
  • The stress often moves: JApan → japaNESE · Italy → iTAlian · Egypt → eGYPtian
  • Use the with some countries: the UK, the Netherlands, the USA, the Philippines.
  • A nationality word can be an adjective (Thai food) and often the name of a language too (She speaks Thai).

Grammar

Present perfect for experiences · Question tags
Grammar

We use the present perfect (have/has + past participle) to talk about life experiences. These are things that happened at some time in our lives. We don't say when.

➕ Positive

Subjecthave / has
I / You / We / Theyhave ('ve)been to Japan.
He / She / Ithas ('s)been to Japan.

➖ Negative

I haven't been to Brazil.
She hasn't been to Brazil.
I've never been to Brazil.

never = not at any time in my life. Don't use it with haven't: I haven't never…

❓ Questions & short answers

Have you ever been to Egypt?
→ Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
Has he ever been to Spain?
→ Yes, he has. / No, he hasn't.

ever = at any time in your life (only in questions)

🔀 Present perfect → past simple

Start with the experience, then give details with the past simple:

A: Have you ever been to Bali?
B: Yes, I have. I went there in 2023.
A: Who did you go with?

✗ I've been to Paris last year.
✓ I went to Paris last year.

✈️ been to vs gone to

She's been to Italy. → She went and came back. 🏠

She's gone to Italy. → She is in Italy now. 🇮🇹

📍 Small words

been to + place: been to London
but no to with there / here / abroad:
✓ I've been there. ✗ I've been to there.
✓ Have you ever been abroad?

🔍 Spot the mistake (click to check)

A question tag is a short question at the end of a sentence. We use it to check information we think is true, or to ask someone to agree with us.
You've been to Japan, haven't you?

⚖️ The golden rule

Positive sentence → negative tag
You're Indonesian, aren't you?

Negative sentence → positive tag
You aren't French, are you?

Tag = auxiliary verb + pronoun (not a name!)
Tom is Australian, isn't he? ✗ isn't Tom?

🧩 Which auxiliary? Use the verb from the sentence.

Tense / verbExample
beThai food is spicy, isn't it?
Present simpleYou live in Jakarta, don't you? · She speaks Thai, doesn't she?
Past simpleThey went to Rome, didn't they?
Present perfectYou've been to Japan, haven't you? · He hasn't been to Egypt, has he?
can / willYou can speak Spanish, can't you? · It will be hot, won't it?

⭐ Special cases

I'm right, aren't I? (not amn't I)

Let's go to Spain, shall we?

never is negative →
You've never been to Paris, have you?

🎵 Intonation changes the meaning

↘ Falling

It's beautiful, isn't it? ↘

I'm sure. I just want you to agree. (= chatting)

↗ Rising

You've been to Kyoto, haven't you? ↗

I'm not sure. It's a real question.

One Backpack, Five Countries

Have you ever been to a country where you can't read a single sign? This year I have – five times! My name is Lina. I'm Indonesian and I live in Yogyakarta, but in January I packed one small backpack and started the biggest adventure of my life.

My first stop was Japan. I spent two weeks in Kyoto, visiting temples and eating more ramen than I want to admit. One evening an old Japanese woman sat next to me on the bus. “You're not from here, are you?” she asked with a smile. “No, I'm from Indonesia,” I said. “Ah! I've been to Bali twice. It's beautiful, isn't it?” We talked all the way to the station.

After Japan, I flew to Hanoi. Vietnamese coffee is strong and sweet, and I drank it every morning while I watched thousands of motorbikes go past. Then I went to Istanbul, a city that is half in Europe and half in Asia. The Turkish breakfast was my favourite meal of the whole trip: bread, cheese, olives, eggs, honey and endless glasses of tea.

In Italy I travelled by train from Rome to Florence. Italian is a bit like Spanish, so I could understand a few words, but people spoke very fast! My last country was Portugal. In Lisbon, I met two Australian girls in my hostel. “You've been to Indonesia, haven't you?” I asked one of them, because she had a T-shirt from Bali. “Not yet!” she laughed. “My sister has, though. She bought me this shirt.”

Now I'm home again, and everyone asks me, “Which country was the best?” Honestly, I can't choose. My best friend Dewi has never been abroad, but next month she's going to visit Thailand with me. And my brother? He has gone to Australia to study, so he's in Melbourne right now and won't be back until December.

If you have never travelled alone, try it at least once. You will meet amazing people and learn a lot about yourself. The world is a big classroom, isn't it?

Speaking

Monologue with a cue card · IELTS Part 2 style
Speaking
Cue card

Describe a country or city you have visited that you would like to visit again.

You should say:
  • where it is
  • when you went there and who you went with
  • what you did and saw there
and explain why you would like to go back.

You have 1 minute to prepare. You can make notes. Then talk for 1–2 minutes. Haven't travelled abroad? Talk about a city in your own country.

  • Do you think people will travel more or less in the future? Why?

Writing

A blog post about your favourite place
Writing

Task: Write a blog post (120–180 words) called “The best place I've ever been to”. Write about your favourite country, city or place you have visited. Include:

  • other places you have been to (present perfect)
  • when you went, who with, and what you did (past simple)
  • at least two nationality words
  • one question tag to talk to your reader
  • why it's your favourite, and whether you recommend it

Model: The best place I've ever been to

I've been to quite a few places in Asia, like Singapore and Malaysia, but the best place I've ever been to is Chiang Mai in the north of Thailand.

I went there last December with my two best friends. We stayed in a small guesthouse in the old city, and the owner was a really friendly Thai woman called Malee.

Every evening we walked around the night markets and tried different dishes. The khao soi was amazing! We also visited old temples and took a cooking class with a Canadian couple and two German students. It was so much fun.

Why is it my favourite? The people were kind, the food was cheap and delicious, and the city felt calm, not crowded like Bangkok. You love trying new food, don't you? Then you should definitely go. I've never felt so relaxed on holiday!
