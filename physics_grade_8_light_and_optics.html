Does light travel in a straight line or curve?
Straight line (rectilinear propagation) — unless it passes through a new medium.
👁️
Why can we see objects that don't emit light?
Light reflects off them (diffuse reflection) and enters our eyes.
🥄
What happens when light hits a mirror?
It bounces back — this is reflection. Angle in = angle out (from the normal).
🥤
Why does a straw look bent in water?
Refraction — light bends when it changes medium (air → water) because its speed changes.
⚡
How fast does light travel in vacuum?
c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (~300,000 km/s). Nothing travels faster.
🌈
What causes a rainbow?
Refraction + internal reflection inside water droplets, splitting white light into colours.

Part A · 20 min
Light & Optics
The nature of light, how it reflects, and how it bends when changing medium.
01 What Is Light?
▾

Light is a form of electromagnetic radiation visible to the human eye. Key facts:

  • Travels in a straight line — rectilinear propagation
  • Does not need a medium (travels through vacuum)
  • Speed in vacuum: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  • Slows down in denser media (glass, water)
  • Has both wave and particle properties (wave-particle duality)
💡 Why do shadows have sharp edges? Because light travels in straight lines — it can't bend around corners (unlike sound).
02 Reflection of Light
▾

The Law of Reflection: The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection — both measured from the normal (a perpendicular line at the point of contact).

θᵢ = θᵣangle of incidence = angle of reflection (from the normal)
TypeSurfaceResult
SpecularSmooth (mirror, calm water)Clear, ordered reflection
DiffuseRough (paper, walls, skin)Scattered in many directions
🧠 We see most objects because of diffuse reflection — not because they emit light or shine like mirrors.
03 Refraction & Snell's Law
▾

Refraction occurs when light changes speed as it moves from one medium to another, causing it to bend.

n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂Snell's Law: n = refractive index, θ = angle from normal
n = c / vRefractive index = speed of light in vacuum ÷ speed in medium
MediumnLight speed
Vacuum1.003.00 × 10⁸ m/s
Air~1.00≈ vacuum

Glass~1.52.00 × 10⁸ m/s
Diamond2.421.24 × 10⁸ m/s

Rule: Higher n → light slows more → bends more toward the normal.

🌊 When moving into a denser medium (n increases), light bends toward the normal. Into a less dense medium, it bends away.

04 Total Internal Reflection
▾

When light travels from a denser → less dense medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle (θc), the light cannot escape — it reflects entirely back.

sin θc = n₂ / n₁Critical angle formula (where n₁ > n₂)

Applications: optical fibres (internet cables), endoscopes, diamond sparkle.

💎 Diamond's high n (2.42) gives a very small critical angle (~24°), so light bounces internally many times before escaping — creating that dazzling sparkle.

02 Types of Mirrors
▾

🪞 Plane Mirror

  • Flat reflective surface
  • Image always virtual
  • Always upright
  • Same size as object (m = 1)
  • Image distance = object distance
  • Laterally inverted (left↔right)

📍 Applications

  • Bathroom mirrors
  • Periscopes (two plane mirrors)
  • Dressing mirrors
  • Laser beam direction

🔵 Concave Mirror — Converging

Curves inward (like the inside of a bowl). Parallel rays reflect through the focal point F.

04 Worked Example — Step by Step
▾

Problem: An object is placed 30 cm in front of a concave mirror with focal length 10 cm. Find the image distance, magnification, and describe the image.

1
Write down knowns: u = −30 cm, f = −10 cm (concave → negative)
2
Apply mirror equation: 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/(−10) − 1/(−30) = −3/30 + 1/30 = −2/30
3
v = −15 cm — negative means the image is in front of the mirror → Real image
4
Magnification: m = −v/u = −(−15)/(−30) = −0.5 → Inverted, diminished (half size)
5
Answer: Image is 15 cm in front of mirror, real, inverted, and 0.5× the size of the object.

Problem 1
An object is placed 40 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Calculate (a) image distance, (b) magnification, (c) describe the image fully.
▸ show answer key
f = −15 cm, u = −40 cm → 1/v = 1/(−15) − 1/(−40) = −8/120 + 3/120 = −5/120 → v = −24 cm
m = −(−24)/(−40) = −0.6
Image: Real (v negative), Inverted (m negative), Diminished (|m| = 0.6 < 1)
Problem 2
A convex mirror has radius of curvature 60 cm. An object is 45 cm in front of it. Find (a) focal length, (b) image distance, (c) state whether real or virtual and explain why.
▸ show answer key
f = R/2 = 60/2 = +30 cm (convex → positive)
u = −45 cm → 1/v = 1/30 − 1/(−45) = 1/30 + 1/45 = 5/90 → v = +18 cm
Virtual — positive v means image forms behind the mirror (virtual side).
Problem 3
A concave mirror produces a real image on a screen 60 cm from the mirror. The object is 20 cm away. Find (a) focal length, (b) magnification, (c) image height if object is 5 cm tall.
▸ show answer key
Real image on screen → v = −60 cm, u = −20 cm
1/f = 1/(−60) + 1/(−20) = −4/60 → f = −15 cm
m = −(−60)/(−20) = −3 (inverted, 3× larger)
hᵢ = m × h₀ = −3 × 5 = −15 cm (15 cm tall, inverted)

Section C — Critical Thinking

Critical Thinking 1
A student says: "Convex mirrors are useless — they make everything smaller." Do you agree or disagree? Justify using both advantages and limitations of convex mirrors.
▸ show guide
Disagree. The diminished image is a trade-off for a wider field of view. In safety contexts (vehicle mirrors, road corners, security), seeing more area matters more than seeing larger. A convex mirror covers what a flat mirror cannot — this is its design advantage, not a flaw. It is unsuitable for magnification tasks, but that is not its purpose.
Critical Thinking 2
As an object moves from beyond C toward F in a concave mirror, describe how the image changes in position, size, and orientation. What happens exactly at F?
▸ show guide
Beyond C → image between F and C (real, inverted, smaller). At C → image at C (real, inverted, same size). Between F and C → image beyond C (real, inverted, increasingly larger). As object approaches F → image moves toward infinity, size grows without bound. At F → reflected rays are parallel, no convergence point, image at infinity.
