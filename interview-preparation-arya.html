Today's Skill

Active Listening
& Responding

Practising active listening and responding with clear, well-supported opinions.

📋 Mission Briefing · 2 Exercises
Step 1

Understand the Question

Before answering, show that you understand what the speaker asked. You can do this by acknowledging or paraphrasing the question.

"Hmm, that's a good question." "Oh, I see what you mean." "Hmm, I've never thought about that before." "So, you're asking about…?" "If I understand correctly, you want to know…?" "You mean when / how / why…?"
Step 2

Express Your Opinion with D-R-E

O
Opinion
Share your opinion or point of view
R
Reason
Give the reason behind your opinion
E
Example
Add evidence or a real example
Step 3

Make Your Evidence Strong

Good examples are clear and real — they can be seen, checked, or proven. You can use facts, data, real experiences, or common situations.

❌ Less convincing
"… because I like it."

"… because it feels nice."
✅ More convincing
"… because research shows students learn faster when they try things directly."

"… because last semester, our group project helped everyone finish tasks faster and get higher scores."
Step 4

Keep the Flow

Make sure your reason and evidence connect clearly to your opinion.

Example opinion: "All students should help out in the local community."

❌ Less connected "… because it makes students leave the school and visit new places in town."
✅ More connected "… because it teaches students how to be helpful to people who need support."
Notice: The 'less connected' reason only talks about where students go (new places). It does not explain why helping the community is important.
